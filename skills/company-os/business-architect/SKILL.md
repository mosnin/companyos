# Business Architect

This is a thinking overlay for business-architecture Sol managers. It does not own authority, leases, fabric, or completion. `$manage-company-program`, `$strategy-pillar`, and `$portfolio-direction` still win.

This doctrine is an original compiled operating template stored with its source pack. It is not a claim of personal identity, private knowledge, quoted speech, or affiliation with McKinsey, Porter, Jobs, or Strategyzer.

Do not send this skill to Luna workers. Do not use it as the master persona. Do not attach it to a manager whose outcome is not business model, competitive strategy, value proposition, jobs-to-be-done, or market analysis.

## Every heartbeat

Before dispatching business-architecture work, answer these:

1. Name the decision. A canvas without a decision is decoration.
2. Pick one primary framework. Do not stack all seventeen invokers.
3. Never invent business facts. Mark assumptions and the test that would falsify them.
4. Keep one customer, job, or segment unless the framework is industry-level.
5. Pause if this is a canvas without a decision, a strategy without a constraint, or a metric without a customer-value link.

Do not invent customers, TAM, legal conclusions, or competitor facts. Do not authorize campaigns, offers, CRM, HRIS, scheduler, or runtime changes from this overlay.

## Forced moves

- Lean Canvas for early or uncertain work; Business Model Canvas when the model is more known.
- Value Proposition Canvas is one segment only. JTBD uses: When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [outcome].
- Industry tools stay at industry level. Do not fake firm-level Five Forces or PESTEL data.
- North Star must reflect customer value and carry guardrails.
- Name the bottleneck and the cheapest test that would kill the model.
- Do not enable the Company OS scheduler or runtime from this overlay.

## Artifacts

Keep these manager-local and compact:

- named decision and chosen framework
- filled canvas or force map with assumptions ledger
- bottleneck, risks, and falsifying tests
- one leading metric with guardrails when a metric is the decision
