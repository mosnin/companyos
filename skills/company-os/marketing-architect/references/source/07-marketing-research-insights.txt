MARKETING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 07: Marketing Research and Insight Methods: Quant, Qual, TURF, Conjoint

Purpose
Design and execute marketing systems that create demand, capture value, and compound brand equity.
This doctrine is for decision-making and execution: market selection, segmentation, positioning, messaging, channels,
campaigns, research, measurement, and iteration.

Core Model
1) Market: where value can be captured (demand, competition, willingness to pay).
2) Offer: value proposition + proof + pricing and packaging.
3) Message: positioning translated into clear, testable claims.
4) Distribution: channels and loops that deliver attention and conversion.
5) Measurement: feedback that prevents self-deception.

Operating Rules
1) Start with market structure, then customers, then message, then channels.
2) Targeting and positioning are choices; clarity beats breadth.
3) Behavior beats opinions. Experiments beat debates.
4) Attribution is imperfect; design measurement to survive uncertainty.
5) Brand is a long-term asset; performance is short-term optimization. Balance both.

============================================================
Scope
Research reduces uncertainty. Use it to decide segments, messages, offers, and channel bets.

1) Qualitative Research
Use for: motivations, language, objections, context.
Methods
• interviews
• observation and ethnography
• diary studies
• concept and message tests
Outputs
• verbatim language bank
• objection list
• decision criteria map

2) Quantitative Research
Use for: prevalence, segmentation, measurement, forecasting.
Methods
• surveys
• behavioral data analysis
• experiments
Outputs
• segment sizes
• driver analysis
• quantified preference structures

3) TURF Analysis
Total Unduplicated Reach and Frequency
Use to select a set of benefits or features that maximizes coverage across preferences.

4) Conjoint Analysis
Use to measure attribute tradeoffs (including price).
Outputs
• utilities per attribute level
• share simulations for packaging/pricing options

5) Causal-Comparative Research
Compare existing groups where randomization is not possible.
Rule
Control confounds where possible, and label conclusions as associative unless design supports causality.

6) Cross Tabulation
Compare responses by segment and source:
• segment × value driver
• channel × objection
• cohort × retention

7) Research Hygiene
1) define the decision first
2) avoid leading questions
3) ensure sampling matches the target population
4) validate with behavioral evidence where possible
5) report uncertainty and limitations

Deliverables
1) research plan by decision question
2) qual insight brief and language bank
3) quant segment map with cross-tabs
4) TURF or conjoint outputs when appropriate
5) next experiments derived from findings
