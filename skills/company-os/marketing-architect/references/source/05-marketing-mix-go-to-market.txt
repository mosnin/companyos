MARKETING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 05: Marketing Mix and Go-to-Market Design: 4Ps, 7Ps, Channel Strategy

Purpose
Design and execute marketing systems that create demand, capture value, and compound brand equity.
This doctrine is for decision-making and execution: market selection, segmentation, positioning, messaging, channels,
campaigns, research, measurement, and iteration.

Core Model
1) Market: where value can be captured (demand, competition, willingness to pay).
2) Offer: value proposition + proof + pricing and packaging.
3) Message: positioning translated into clear, testable claims.
4) Distribution: channels and loops that deliver attention and conversion.
5) Measurement: feedback that prevents self-deception.

Operating Rules
1) Start with market structure, then customers, then message, then channels.
2) Targeting and positioning are choices; clarity beats breadth.
3) Behavior beats opinions. Experiments beat debates.
4) Attribution is imperfect; design measurement to survive uncertainty.
5) Brand is a long-term asset; performance is short-term optimization. Balance both.

============================================================
Scope
Translate strategy into a coherent system: product, pricing, placement, promotion plus service elements.

1) 4Ps and 7Ps
4Ps
1) Product: features, packaging, quality, variants
2) Price: list price, discounts, bundling, financing, terms
3) Place: distribution channels, logistics, availability
4) Promotion: messaging, media, sales enablement

7Ps (services and experience)
5) People: sales, support, community, training
6) Process: delivery, onboarding, fulfillment, service recovery
7) Physical evidence: brand cues, proof assets, environment, UI

2) Channel Strategy (Where demand is captured)
Channel types
1) Owned: email list, content, community, SEO
2) Paid: search, social, display, affiliates
3) Earned: PR, reviews, word-of-mouth, partnerships
4) Sales-led: outbound, partnerships, channel sales

Selection criteria
1) target presence and intent
2) unit economics (CAC, payback)
3) creative requirements and speed
4) measurement feasibility
5) scalability and saturation risk

3) Offer Design and Packaging
Offer = value proposition + terms + risk reversal + bonuses + constraints.
Packaging levers
• tiers and bundles
• usage limits
• add-ons and upgrades
• trials, guarantees, financing

4) Growth Hacking as Controlled Experimentation
Rapid hypothesis testing across AARRR:
acquisition, activation, retention, referral, revenue.
Rule
Every “hack” has a metric, guardrails, and a stop rule.

Deliverables
1) 7Ps mapping
2) channel strategy table
3) offer and packaging map
4) experiment backlog for channels and offers
