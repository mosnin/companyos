MARKETING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 01: Market Strategy Foundations: Markets, Competition, Advantage

Purpose
Design and execute marketing systems that create demand, capture value, and compound brand equity.
This doctrine is for decision-making and execution: market selection, segmentation, positioning, messaging, channels,
campaigns, research, measurement, and iteration.

Core Model
1) Market: where value can be captured (demand, competition, willingness to pay).
2) Offer: value proposition + proof + pricing and packaging.
3) Message: positioning translated into clear, testable claims.
4) Distribution: channels and loops that deliver attention and conversion.
5) Measurement: feedback that prevents self-deception.

Operating Rules
1) Start with market structure, then customers, then message, then channels.
2) Targeting and positioning are choices; clarity beats breadth.
3) Behavior beats opinions. Experiments beat debates.
4) Attribution is imperfect; design measurement to survive uncertainty.
5) Brand is a long-term asset; performance is short-term optimization. Balance both.

============================================================
Scope
Market selection and competitive structure determine how hard marketing will be.
This section covers: defining markets, supply and demand, comparative advantage, business models, competitive analysis,
Porter’s Five Forces, SWOT, 7Cs Compass, Ansoff Matrix, Blue Ocean Strategy, and economic divides.

1) Defining the Market (Do Not Skip)
A “market” is a specific set of buyers with similar jobs, constraints, and willingness to pay.
Define by:
1) Buyer: who pays and who uses
2) Job: progress they want
3) Context: when and why the job becomes urgent
4) Alternatives: how they solve it today
5) Pricing bands: what is “normal” spend and why

Deliverable: Market Definition Card
• Segment name
• Job and urgency triggers
• Buyer and user
• Alternatives
• Price anchors and buying friction
• Distribution channels that reach them

2) Supply, Demand, and Elasticity
Basics
• Demand curve: willingness to pay at different prices
• Supply curve: willingness and ability to provide at different costs
• Elasticity: how price affects quantity demanded
Marketing implications
1) In elastic categories, differentiation and value communication matter more
2) In inelastic categories, availability, trust, and distribution dominate

3) Comparative Advantage (Strategic Edge)
Comparative advantage is what you can do at lower opportunity cost than competitors.
Sources
1) Distribution advantage: cheaper attention or better access
2) Production advantage: lower costs, higher quality, faster delivery
3) Information advantage: better data, better targeting
4) Brand advantage: trust, preference, recall
5) Switching advantage: lock-in, integration, habit

4) Business Models and Economic Divides
Common models
1) Transactional (one-time purchase)
2) Subscription (recurring)
3) Usage-based
4) Marketplace and platform
5) Freemium
6) Services and retainers
7) Licensing
Marketing must match model:
• Subscription: retention, activation, habit
• Marketplace: liquidity, trust, supply and demand balancing
• Services: proof, authority, risk reversal, sales process

5) Competitive Structure Tools
A) Porter’s Five Forces
1) Rivalry
2) Threat of substitutes
3) Threat of new entrants
4) Buyer power
5) Supplier power
Use: identify where profit is captured and where marketing must compensate.

B) SWOT (Marketing Use)
• Strengths and weaknesses: internal capabilities and constraints
• Opportunities and threats: external market forces
Use: to pick strategies you can actually execute.

C) 7Cs Compass Model (Orientation)
Customer, Competitor, Company
Complementors, Collaborators
Context, Channels
Use: ensure you are not missing ecosystem forces.

6) Strategy Frameworks for Growth
A) Ansoff Matrix
1) Market penetration
2) Market development
3) Product development
4) Diversification
Use: choose growth vector and risk level.

B) Blue Ocean Strategy
Goal: shift from competing in a crowded space to creating uncontested demand.
Levers: eliminate, reduce, raise, create (ERRC grid).

Deliverables
1) Market definition card
2) Porter + 7Cs snapshot
3) SWOT tied to strategy choices
4) Ansoff growth path selection
5) Blue Ocean ERRC grid (optional)
