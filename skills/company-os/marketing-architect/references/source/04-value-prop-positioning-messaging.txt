MARKETING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 04: Value Proposition, Positioning, and Messaging Architecture

Purpose
Design and execute marketing systems that create demand, capture value, and compound brand equity.
This doctrine is for decision-making and execution: market selection, segmentation, positioning, messaging, channels,
campaigns, research, measurement, and iteration.

Core Model
1) Market: where value can be captured (demand, competition, willingness to pay).
2) Offer: value proposition + proof + pricing and packaging.
3) Message: positioning translated into clear, testable claims.
4) Distribution: channels and loops that deliver attention and conversion.
5) Measurement: feedback that prevents self-deception.

Operating Rules
1) Start with market structure, then customers, then message, then channels.
2) Targeting and positioning are choices; clarity beats breadth.
3) Behavior beats opinions. Experiments beat debates.
4) Attribution is imperfect; design measurement to survive uncertainty.
5) Brand is a long-term asset; performance is short-term optimization. Balance both.

============================================================
Scope
This section turns customer truth into differentiated messaging that can be tested.

1) Value and Value Proposition
Value = outcomes achieved minus costs (money, time, risk, effort, identity).
Value Proposition components
1) Target customer
2) Job and desired outcomes
3) Differentiated mechanism (why you, why now)
4) Proof (evidence)
5) Offer structure (pricing, terms, guarantees)

2) Positioning (Choice, Not Description)
Positioning defines:
• category or frame of reference
• primary competitor or alternative
• unique benefit
• reason to believe
• who it is not for

Positioning Statement Template
“For [target], who [job], [brand] is the [category] that [unique benefit] because [reason to believe]. Unlike [alternative], we [key differentiator].”

3) Differentiation Types
1) Performance: better results
2) Convenience: faster, easier, fewer steps
3) Reliability: lower risk, fewer failures
4) Cost: better value per unit outcome
5) Identity: signals status or tribe
6) Distribution: availability and access
7) Service: experience and support
Pick 1 primary, 1 secondary.

4) Messaging Architecture (System)
Levels
1) Core message: one sentence claim
2) Supporting pillars: 3 to 5 proof-backed themes
3) Proof assets: case studies, data, demos, testimonials
4) Objection handling: risk reversal and explanations
5) CTA ladder: micro-commitments to purchase

5) Persuasion Language and “God Terms”
Use high-impact terms carefully and tie them to proof.
Rule
Avoid unprovable claims. Convert claims into measurable promises or framed hypotheses for tests.

Deliverables
1) value proposition one-pager
2) positioning statement
3) messaging architecture: core + pillars + proof + objections
4) CTA ladder: low to high commitment
5) claim test plan: where and how each claim is validated
