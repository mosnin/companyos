MARKETING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 02: Segmentation, Targeting, Positioning Inputs: Personas and Psychographics

Purpose
Design and execute marketing systems that create demand, capture value, and compound brand equity.
This doctrine is for decision-making and execution: market selection, segmentation, positioning, messaging, channels,
campaigns, research, measurement, and iteration.

Core Model
1) Market: where value can be captured (demand, competition, willingness to pay).
2) Offer: value proposition + proof + pricing and packaging.
3) Message: positioning translated into clear, testable claims.
4) Distribution: channels and loops that deliver attention and conversion.
5) Measurement: feedback that prevents self-deception.

Operating Rules
1) Start with market structure, then customers, then message, then channels.
2) Targeting and positioning are choices; clarity beats breadth.
3) Behavior beats opinions. Experiments beat debates.
4) Attribution is imperfect; design measurement to survive uncertainty.
5) Brand is a long-term asset; performance is short-term optimization. Balance both.

============================================================
Scope
This section builds target audiences and personas using demographics, psychographics, needs/wants/desires,
and the STP framework.

1) STP Framework
A) Segmentation
Partition the market into groups that differ in:
• Jobs and outcomes
• Willingness to pay
• Decision criteria
• Channel behavior and reachability
• Risk tolerance and trust needs

B) Targeting
Select segments based on:
1) attractiveness: size, growth, margins, accessibility
2) fit: capabilities, distribution edge, brand credibility
3) defensibility: moats, switching costs, differentiation

C) Positioning inputs (full positioning is Section 04)
Choose a unique “reason to choose” for the target segment.

2) Persona and Buyer Roles
Separate:
• Economic buyer: budget holder
• Champion: internal promoter
• User: daily operator
• Influencer: shapes criteria
• Gatekeeper: controls access

Persona Template (Execution Grade)
1) Context: environment, constraints, timeline
2) Trigger: what makes them search now
3) Job: functional, emotional, social
4) Success: outcomes and metrics they care about
5) Decision criteria: what must be true to buy
6) Objections: risks, doubts, deal-breakers
7) Channels: where they pay attention
8) Language: the words they use

3) Psychographics
Psychographics are patterns of:
• values and identity
• motivations and fears
• lifestyle and habits
• status games and tribe membership
• worldview and beliefs about “good” solutions

How to collect psychographics
1) interviews and call notes
2) review mining (forums, reviews, social comments)
3) content consumption mapping
4) cohort analysis by behavior

4) Customer Profile: Needs, Wants, Desires
Operational definitions
• Needs: non-negotiable requirements to make progress
• Wants: preferred attributes and convenience
• Desires: identity, status, aspiration, emotion

Deliverable: Needs-Wants-Desires Ladder
• Must-haves (needs)
• Nice-to-haves (wants)
• Identity levers (desires)
Tie each to proof and messaging.

5) Segment Design Checklist
A segment is usable when:
1) you can describe it in one sentence
2) you can find and reach it cheaply
3) it has a distinct “why buy” and “why now”
4) you can win with your capabilities

Deliverables
1) Segmentation map with criteria
2) Target segment selection with rationale
3) Persona set by buyer role
4) Needs-Wants-Desires ladder
5) Channel reach map per segment
