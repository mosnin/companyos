MARKETING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 03: Buyer Behavior and the Decision Journey: AIDA, Funnels, Post-Purchase

Purpose
Design and execute marketing systems that create demand, capture value, and compound brand equity.
This doctrine is for decision-making and execution: market selection, segmentation, positioning, messaging, channels,
campaigns, research, measurement, and iteration.

Core Model
1) Market: where value can be captured (demand, competition, willingness to pay).
2) Offer: value proposition + proof + pricing and packaging.
3) Message: positioning translated into clear, testable claims.
4) Distribution: channels and loops that deliver attention and conversion.
5) Measurement: feedback that prevents self-deception.

Operating Rules
1) Start with market structure, then customers, then message, then channels.
2) Targeting and positioning are choices; clarity beats breadth.
3) Behavior beats opinions. Experiments beat debates.
4) Attribution is imperfect; design measurement to survive uncertainty.
5) Brand is a long-term asset; performance is short-term optimization. Balance both.

============================================================
Scope
Marketing must match how people decide. This section covers the buyer decision process and behavior types.

1) Buyer Decision Process
Stages
1) Problem/need recognition
2) Information search
3) Evaluation of alternatives
4) Purchase decision
5) Post-purchase behavior (use, satisfaction, churn, advocacy)

For each stage define:
• questions in the buyer’s mind
• proof required
• dominant risks
• best channels and assets

2) Buyer Behavior Types (Consumer Decision Modes)
1) Habitual buying behavior
   • low involvement; rely on availability and familiarity
2) Variety seeking
   • low involvement but switching for novelty; focus on differentiation, packaging, refresh cycles
3) Complex buying behavior
   • high involvement; long evaluation; needs deep proof, comparisons, demos
4) Dissonance-reducing
   • high involvement but hard to differentiate; needs reassurance, guarantees, post-purchase support

3) AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)
Use as a copy and funnel diagnostic, not as a rigid model.
Mapping
• Attention: hooks, targeting, relevance
• Interest: clarity, credibility, problem framing
• Desire: differentiation, proof, value stack
• Action: friction removal, risk reversal, CTA strength

4) Funnel Architecture (Practical)
Think in three layers:
1) Awareness: reach and recall
2) Consideration: education and proof
3) Conversion: offer, pricing, risk reversal, friction reduction
Then add:
4) Retention: onboarding, habit, success outcomes
5) Referral: share loops, advocacy assets

Deliverable: Journey Map
• stage, intent, questions, objections
• best asset type per stage (ad, landing page, email, case study, demo, trial)
• measurement per stage

5) Post-Purchase Behavior as Marketing
Post-purchase drives:
• reviews, referrals, LTV, and brand equity
Tools
1) onboarding sequences
2) success milestones
3) community and support
4) NPS and review asks at high-satisfaction moments

Deliverables
1) stage-by-stage intent map
2) objections library by stage
3) asset map and content plan by stage
4) retention and referral loop design
