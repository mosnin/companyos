OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 07: Inventory Management Methods: EOQ, ROP, Safety Stock, ABC, FIFO/LIFO, JIT

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

============================================================
Scope
Policy toolkit for inventory control and replenishment.

1) ABC Analysis
Rank by annual usage value or impact.
A: tight control, frequent review, higher service level.
B: moderate.
C: simple controls.

2) EOQ Baseline
EOQ ≈ sqrt((2 × D × S) / H)
D annual demand; S fixed ordering or setup cost; H annual holding cost per unit.
Use as baseline; adjust for variability, lead time, and constraints.

3) Reorder Point and Safety Stock
ROP = demand during lead time + safety stock
Baseline demand during lead time = d × L
Safety stock reflects uncertainty and service level target.
Practical method
1) choose service level by ABC class
2) estimate sigma of demand during lead time
3) safety stock = z × sigma_LT

4) FIFO and LIFO
FIFO reduces spoilage and obsolescence in physical flow.
LIFO is primarily accounting and may not match physical flow.

5) Batch Tracking, Traceability, and Consignment
Use batch tracking for recalls, compliance, or supplier variability.
Consignment shifts ownership to supplier until use, improving cash but increasing coordination.

6) Cross-Docking and Dropshipping
Cross-docking reduces storage, increases coordination requirements.
Dropshipping reduces owned inventory, may reduce control and speed.

7) JIT and Lean Replenishment
Requires stable processes, reliable suppliers, short setups, strong quality.

8) Perpetual Inventory
Continuous tracking suited to fast operations and high-value items.

Deliverables
1) ABC and service level policy
2) EOQ baseline and adjustments
3) ROP and safety stock per item class
4) replenishment model choice by SKU category
5) traceability requirements
