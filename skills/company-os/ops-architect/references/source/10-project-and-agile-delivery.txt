OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 10: Project Management and Agile Delivery: CPM, Resources, Risk, Scrum

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

============================================================
Scope
Execute operational change through projects and delivery cadences.

Part A: Project Management
1) Initiation
Outputs: charter, scope, success criteria, stakeholders, constraints, assumptions, risks.

2) Scheduling and Critical Path
Build AON network; compute critical path and slack; protect critical activities.

3) Duration Uncertainty
Use ranges where possible; avoid single-point illusions.

4) Resource Constraints
Schedules are only real with resources.
Level resources; reduce multitasking; enforce WIP limits on projects.

5) Risk and Cost Management
Maintain risk register: probability, impact, triggers, mitigation, contingency, owner.
Track baseline budget, committed, actual, forecast at completion.

6) Closing
Acceptance criteria met; handoff to operations with SOPs; lessons learned; post-launch metrics.

Part B: Agile and Scrum
1) Waterfall vs Agile
Waterfall fits stable requirements; Agile fits learning and iteration.

2) Scrum Elements
Roles: product owner, scrum master, team.
Artifacts: backlog, sprint goal, definition of done, increment.
Events: planning, daily sync, review, retrospective.

3) Tracking Progress
Use burn down, cycle time, throughput, and quality metrics, not only percent complete.

Deliverables
1) project charter and AON schedule
2) critical path plus buffers
3) resource leveling plan
4) risk register and cost dashboard
5) scrum cadence and backlog policy
