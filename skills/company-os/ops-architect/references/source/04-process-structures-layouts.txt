OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 04: Process Structures and Layouts: Job, Batch, Line, Continuous Flow

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

============================================================
Scope
Process structure determines cost, flexibility, throughput, and lead time.

1) Job Shop
High variety, low volume; functional departments; variable routing.
Pros: flexibility, customization.
Cons: complex scheduling, high WIP, long lead times under load.
Levers: standardize routings, reduce setups, strong dispatch rules, WIP limits.

2) Batch Shop
Work processed in lots; setup costs encourage batching.
Pros: setup efficiency, scale economies.
Cons: waiting between batches, obsolescence risk.
Levers: reduce setup time, shrink batch sizes, prioritize by due dates and constraint minutes.

3) Flow Line and Worker-Paced Assembly
Sequential steps; people pace the line.
Pros: predictable flow, simpler planning.
Cons: balancing sensitivity; stoppages propagate.
Levers: line balancing, small buffers at joins, cross-training.

4) Machine-Paced Assembly Line
Mechanized pace and takt time.
Pros: high throughput, consistency.
Cons: low flexibility, high disruption cost.
Levers: maintenance, upstream quality, redundancy at critical equipment.

5) Continuous Flow
Very high volume, continuous processing.
Pros: very low unit cost at scale.
Cons: high switching cost; downtime is expensive.
Levers: reliability engineering, process control.

6) Selection Logic
Choose based on
1) volume and variety
2) demand and processing variability
3) customization requirements
4) capital and skill availability
5) lead time targets
6) quality risk

Deliverables
1) current structure diagnosis
2) target structure and rationale
3) transition plan: batching, setups, layout, scheduling
4) line balance and takt analysis when moving toward flow
