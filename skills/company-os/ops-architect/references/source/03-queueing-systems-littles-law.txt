OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 03: Queueing Systems Design, Queue Discipline, and Little’s Law

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

============================================================
Scope
Queues are inevitable with variability. Design decides where waiting occurs, who waits, and what it costs.

1) Core Terms
1) Arrival process: rate and variability of entries
2) Service process: processing time distribution
3) Servers: parallel resources
4) Queue discipline: rule selecting the next unit

2) Little’s Law (Always Use)
I = R × T
Applications
1) infer missing metric
2) detect inconsistent measurement
3) set WIP limits to hit lead time targets

3) Utilization and Delay
Principle
Waiting grows sharply as utilization rises, especially with variability.
Design implication
Keep critical services below saturation if predictability matters.

4) Queue Disciplines
1) FIFO: stable, fair default
2) LIFO: can reduce abandonment but risks starving old items
3) Priority: use when delay costs differ; require anti-starvation
4) SPT: minimizes average flow time; may be unfair
5) EDD: deadline driven; depends on due date quality

Anti-starvation
1) priority aging
2) service quotas by class
3) time-based guarantees

5) Queue System Architecture
Decisions
1) Single pooled queue vs multiple queues
2) Reservation and appointment systems
3) Triage and routing by class
4) Fast lanes for short jobs when appropriate

6) Batch Effects
Batching reduces setup frequency but increases waiting.
Batch only when it reduces constraint time enough to offset added queueing.

Deliverables
1) Queue map with causes
2) Discipline selection and fairness rules
3) WIP limit policy using Little’s Law
4) Variability reduction plan for arrivals and service times
