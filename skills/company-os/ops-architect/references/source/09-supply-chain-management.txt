OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 09: Supply Chain Management: Sourcing, Logistics, Resilience, and Metrics

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

Scope
Coordinate flows of materials, information, and cash from source to customer.

1) Network View
Nodes: suppliers, production, distribution, customers.
Links: transport, information, payment.

2) Strategic Choices
1) single vs multi sourcing
2) nearshore vs offshore
3) make vs buy
4) centralized vs decentralized inventory
5) push vs pull replenishment

3) Lead Time Anatomy
Includes order processing, supplier queueing, production, transit, receiving.
Reduce variability first; it improves service levels materially.

4) Resilience
Risks
1) supplier failure
2) logistics disruption
3) demand shocks
4) quality failures
5) regulatory and geopolitical

Mechanisms
1) dual sourcing for critical items
2) strategic safety stock
3) flexible capacity agreements
4) substitution and standardization
5) visibility and early warning metrics

5) Metrics
1) OTIF
2) fill rate
3) lead time and variability
4) total landed cost
5) inventory turns
6) cash-to-cash cycle time
7) defect and returns rate

Deliverables
1) supply chain map with tiers and lead times
2) critical item list and controls
3) inventory placement strategy
4) dashboard for OTIF, turns, lead time variability, landed cost
