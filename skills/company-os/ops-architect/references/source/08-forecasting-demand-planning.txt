OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 08: Forecasting and Demand Planning: From Baselines to Bias Control

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

============================================================
Scope
Forecasting reduces uncertainty to improve operational decisions. It is measured by out-of-sample accuracy and bias control.

1) Forecasting Pipeline
1) define demand signal: orders, shipments, consumption
2) clean data: promotions, stockouts, one-offs
3) build baseline model
4) evaluate accuracy and bias
5) add causal inputs only if they improve out-of-sample performance
6) translate forecast into capacity and inventory decisions

2) Baseline Methods
1) naive
2) moving average
3) exponential smoothing
4) seasonal decomposition

Rule
If a complex model cannot beat a baseline out-of-sample, do not use it.

3) Accuracy and Bias
Track both. Bias can be more damaging than moderate error.

4) Uncertainty Bands
Plan with scenarios, not points.
Use P10, P50, P90 bands for inventory and capacity risk control.

5) Bullwhip Control
Mitigations
1) share true demand signals
2) shorten planning cycles
3) stabilize ordering policies
4) reduce batch ordering and long lead times

Deliverables
1) baseline model and evaluation
2) bias tracking and correction rule
3) scenario bands
4) policy translation: how forecasts change inventory and capacity
