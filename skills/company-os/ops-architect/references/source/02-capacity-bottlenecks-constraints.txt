OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 02: Capacity, Bottlenecks, and Constraints Management

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

============================================================
Scope
Bottlenecks determine throughput. Constraints management formalizes how to identify, exploit, and elevate the constraint.

1) Definitions
1) Bottleneck: smallest effective capacity step limiting throughput
2) Constraint: any factor preventing higher throughput (internal or external)
3) Effective capacity: available time divided by total effective time per unit (processing + setup + expected rework + downtime)

2) Bottleneck Detection
Signals
1) Persistent queue before a step
2) High utilization at that step relative to others
3) Downstream starvation and upstream accumulation
4) Small changes at that step change system throughput

Method
1) Compute effective capacity per step
2) Measure WIP and waiting before each step
3) Identify the step gating output rate

3) Theory of Constraints Five Steps
1) Identify the constraint
2) Exploit the constraint (do value work only)
3) Subordinate everything else (align release and pace)
4) Elevate the constraint (add capacity or redesign)
5) Repeat (constraints move)

4) Exploit the Constraint
Policies
1) Protect with a buffer
2) Prioritize mix by contribution per constraint minute
3) Reduce setups at the constraint
4) Remove non-value tasks and prevent rework from reaching the constraint

5) Subordination and Release Control
Principle
Release work based on constraint capacity, not upstream availability.
Mechanisms
1) WIP limits
2) Drum-Buffer-Rope (pace, protection, release rule)

6) Elevation Options
1) Add resources or shifts
2) Reduce processing time
3) Reduce variability via standard work
4) Change mix or route around
5) Parallelize steps
6) Offload via automation or outsourcing

Deliverables
1) Constraint map and evidence
2) Exploitation plan and allowed work definition
3) Release policy with WIP limits
4) Elevation plan with throughput ROI
