OPERATIONS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 06: Inventory Economics: Costs, Benefits, and Policy Tradeoffs

Purpose
Create an expert-level operations playbook for designing, measuring, and improving systems that move work through processes.
This doctrine is built for execution: convert messy reality into flow models, constraints, queue designs, inventory policies,
forecasting systems, supply chain structures, and delivery cadences.

Core Model
1) Work flows through a process; the process has capacity and variability.
2) Variability creates queues; queues create time.
3) Inventory is a buffer against variability, but has costs.
4) Constraints determine throughput; improvement must focus on the constraint.
5) Delivery is governed by network logic (critical path), resource limits, and feedback loops.

Operating Rules
1) Optimize the system, not local steps.
2) Measure flow and variability before changing structure.
3) Improve the bottleneck first; subordinate everything else.
4) Use policies (batching, reorder points, WIP limits) to control variability.
5) Prefer simple, stable policies that survive estimation error.

============================================================
Scope
Inventory buffers variability but ties up capital and can hide problems.

1) Why Inventory Exists
1) decouple mismatched rates and variability
2) protect against demand uncertainty
3) protect against supply lead times
4) exploit ordering, setup, or transport economies
5) meet service level commitments

2) Inventory Costs
1) holding: capital, storage, shrinkage, obsolescence
2) ordering or setup: fixed per replenishment
3) stockout: lost sales, expediting, reputation damage
4) quality: defects stored and propagated
5) complexity: tracking and admin

3) Inventory Benefits
1) higher service level
2) shorter customer lead time
3) reduced changeover frequency when setups are expensive
4) smoothing capacity use

4) Central Tradeoff
More inventory reduces stockouts but increases holding and obsolescence.
Less inventory reduces holding but increases stockout risk.

5) Buffer Placement Rules
1) protect the constraint
2) buffer where variability enters
3) avoid buffers that hide chronic quality issues
4) prefer small, intentional buffers over uncontrolled piles

6) WIP is Inventory
Reducing WIP reduces lead time via Little’s Law.

Deliverables
1) inventory taxonomy and buffers
2) cost model: holding, ordering, stockout, obsolescence
3) target service levels by class
4) buffer placement rationale map
