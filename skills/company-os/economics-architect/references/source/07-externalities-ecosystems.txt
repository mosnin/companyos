ECONOMICS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 07: Externalities, Public Goods, Regulation, and Ecosystems

Purpose
Translate microeconomics and macroeconomics into operating decisions for building and scaling businesses.
This doctrine is action-oriented: pricing, incentives, product strategy, market selection, capacity planning,
competitive dynamics, and macro-aware forecasting.

Core Economic Lens for Business
1) Scarcity and tradeoffs: every choice has an opportunity cost.
2) Incentives and behavior: systems produce outcomes.
3) Margins: decisions happen at the margin (marginal cost vs marginal benefit).
4) Markets: prices coordinate supply and demand under constraints.
5) Information: asymmetric information creates failures and opportunities.
6) Externalities: costs/benefits often spill over beyond the transaction.
7) Cycles: macro conditions shift demand, costs, and risk.

Operating Rules
1) Always define the unit of analysis (customer, order, cohort, market segment, time period).
2) Convert narratives into numbers: demand curves, elasticities, unit economics, and constraints.
3) Separate price from value: price is a lever; value is the driver.
4) Use experiments to estimate uncertain parameters (elasticity, willingness to pay, churn response).
5) Keep models simple enough to guide decisions, strict enough to falsify.

============================================================
Scope
Costs and benefits beyond the buyer-seller pair: externalities and ecosystem design.

1) Externalities
External benefit: others gain from your transaction.
External cost: others bear a cost.
Business examples:
• positive: referrals, community knowledge, network effects
• negative: spam, abuse, misinformation, support burden
Business use:
• amplify positive spillovers
• mitigate negative spillovers with governance

2) Internalizing Externalities
Tools:
• pricing: charge for resource use that creates costs
• rules: enforce standards
• incentives: reward beneficial behavior
• partnerships: align ecosystem incentives

3) Public Goods and Common Resources
Public goods: non-excludable and non-rival.
Common resources: rival but hard to exclude.
Business implications:
• community content can behave like a public good; requires moderation and governance
• attention is a commons; avoid over-messaging and channel fatigue

4) Ecosystem Governance
If you run a platform or community:
• entry standards
• quality enforcement
• dispute resolution
• reputation signals

Deliverables
1) externality map
2) internalization plan (pricing/rules/incentives)
3) ecosystem governance rules
4) risk register for negative externalities
