ECONOMICS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 03: Production and Cost Theory: Inputs, Technology, Capacity, and Unit Economics

Purpose
Translate microeconomics and macroeconomics into operating decisions for building and scaling businesses.
This doctrine is action-oriented: pricing, incentives, product strategy, market selection, capacity planning,
competitive dynamics, and macro-aware forecasting.

Core Economic Lens for Business
1) Scarcity and tradeoffs: every choice has an opportunity cost.
2) Incentives and behavior: systems produce outcomes.
3) Margins: decisions happen at the margin (marginal cost vs marginal benefit).
4) Markets: prices coordinate supply and demand under constraints.
5) Information: asymmetric information creates failures and opportunities.
6) Externalities: costs/benefits often spill over beyond the transaction.
7) Cycles: macro conditions shift demand, costs, and risk.

Operating Rules
1) Always define the unit of analysis (customer, order, cohort, market segment, time period).
2) Convert narratives into numbers: demand curves, elasticities, unit economics, and constraints.
3) Separate price from value: price is a lever; value is the driver.
4) Use experiments to estimate uncertain parameters (elasticity, willingness to pay, churn response).
5) Keep models simple enough to guide decisions, strict enough to falsify.

============================================================
Scope
How firms produce: production functions, costs, and capacity decisions.

1) Production Theory
Production function: output as a function of inputs (labor, capital, tools, process).
Business translation:
• identify key inputs and how output scales with each
• estimate returns to each input (diminishing, constant, increasing)

2) Short Run vs Long Run
Short run: at least one input fixed (capacity constraint).
Long run: all inputs adjustable (you can redesign the system).
Business use:
• short-run optimization: scheduling, throughput, pricing
• long-run optimization: automation, hiring, new tooling, new facilities

3) Cost of Production
Cost types:
• fixed costs: rent, salaried base, tooling subscriptions
• variable costs: COGS, payment fees, fulfillment, commissions
• semi-variable: cloud compute, support headcount
• sunk costs: already incurred; ignore for forward decisions
• opportunity costs: implicit costs of resources you own

4) Average vs Marginal Costs
• average cost (AC): total cost / quantity
• marginal cost (MC): cost of one more unit
Pricing decisions should reference MC and contribution margin, not average cost alone.

5) Unit Economics (Core Business View)
For each unit (order, customer, account):
• revenue per unit
• variable cost per unit
• contribution margin
Then add:
• acquisition cost (CAC)
• retention (churn) and lifetime value (LTV)
Unit economics reveal whether growth compounds or compounds losses.

6) Capacity and Throughput
Capacity is the maximum output per period at acceptable quality.
Constraints:
• labor hours
• machine/compute
• bottleneck steps
Use:
• identify bottleneck input
• invest to increase capacity where MB of capacity > MC

Deliverables
1) production inputs map and returns hypothesis
2) cost taxonomy for your business
3) unit economics model (unit, margin, CAC, LTV)
4) capacity constraint and investment plan
