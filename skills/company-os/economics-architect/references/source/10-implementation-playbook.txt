ECONOMICS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 10: Implementation Playbook: Frameworks, Experiments, and Dashboards

Purpose
Translate microeconomics and macroeconomics into operating decisions for building and scaling businesses.
This doctrine is action-oriented: pricing, incentives, product strategy, market selection, capacity planning,
competitive dynamics, and macro-aware forecasting.

Core Economic Lens for Business
1) Scarcity and tradeoffs: every choice has an opportunity cost.
2) Incentives and behavior: systems produce outcomes.
3) Margins: decisions happen at the margin (marginal cost vs marginal benefit).
4) Markets: prices coordinate supply and demand under constraints.
5) Information: asymmetric information creates failures and opportunities.
6) Externalities: costs/benefits often spill over beyond the transaction.
7) Cycles: macro conditions shift demand, costs, and risk.

Operating Rules
1) Always define the unit of analysis (customer, order, cohort, market segment, time period).
2) Convert narratives into numbers: demand curves, elasticities, unit economics, and constraints.
3) Separate price from value: price is a lever; value is the driver.
4) Use experiments to estimate uncertain parameters (elasticity, willingness to pay, churn response).
5) Keep models simple enough to guide decisions, strict enough to falsify.

============================================================
Scope
Convert economics into repeatable business decisions and artifacts.

1) Utility Framework
Utility = outcomes achieved − (money + time + effort + risk + identity cost)
Implement:
• increase outcomes
• reduce time/effort
• reduce risk with proof and guarantees

2) Supply and Demand in Operations
• map demand drivers by segment
• map supply constraints (capacity, quality)
• use pricing and qualification to balance demand with capacity

3) Pricing Framework
1) choose pricing metric
2) design tiers and fences
3) set discount policy with give-get trades
4) test elasticity
5) review quarterly with cohorts (retention, gross margin)

4) Incentives Framework
1) define desired behavior
2) identify unintended incentives
3) choose incentive type (cash, status, access, autonomy)
4) instrument and monitor
Example: sales incentives tied to retained revenue, not bookings alone.

5) Opportunity Cost Portfolio
Maintain an initiative board:
• expected value
• cost
• time
• risk
Rank by expected value per constrained resource; kill low-return initiatives monthly.

6) Marginal Thinking Applications
• ad spend increments
• hiring increments
• inventory increments
• feature scope increments

7) Macro + Competitor Integration
Macro scenarios plus competitive substitute tracking.
Create budget and hiring triggers.

8) TAM/SAM/SOM to Execution
Use SOM to drive:
• pipeline targets
• capacity targets
• headcount plan
SOM must be feasible given reach and conversion.

Deliverables
1) utility worksheet
2) pricing architecture doc + test plan
3) incentive design checklist
4) initiative portfolio template
5) dashboard spec: unit economics + elasticity + macro watchlist
6) 90-day economic experiment backlog
