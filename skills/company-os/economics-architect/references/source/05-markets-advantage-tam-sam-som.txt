ECONOMICS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 05: Markets, Competition, Advantage, and Market Size (TAM/SAM/SOM)

Purpose
Translate microeconomics and macroeconomics into operating decisions for building and scaling businesses.
This doctrine is action-oriented: pricing, incentives, product strategy, market selection, capacity planning,
competitive dynamics, and macro-aware forecasting.

Core Economic Lens for Business
1) Scarcity and tradeoffs: every choice has an opportunity cost.
2) Incentives and behavior: systems produce outcomes.
3) Margins: decisions happen at the margin (marginal cost vs marginal benefit).
4) Markets: prices coordinate supply and demand under constraints.
5) Information: asymmetric information creates failures and opportunities.
6) Externalities: costs/benefits often spill over beyond the transaction.
7) Cycles: macro conditions shift demand, costs, and risk.

Operating Rules
1) Always define the unit of analysis (customer, order, cohort, market segment, time period).
2) Convert narratives into numbers: demand curves, elasticities, unit economics, and constraints.
3) Separate price from value: price is a lever; value is the driver.
4) Use experiments to estimate uncertain parameters (elasticity, willingness to pay, churn response).
5) Keep models simple enough to guide decisions, strict enough to falsify.

============================================================
Scope
Theory of markets and competitive advantage applied to business strategy.

1) Theory of Markets
A market consists of:
• buyers (demand side)
• sellers (supply side)
• price mechanism
• factors of production (labor, capital, land, entrepreneurship/tech)
Business use:
• define market boundaries using buyer job and substitutes
• identify who has pricing power and why

2) Absolute Advantage, Comparative Advantage, Specialization
Absolute advantage: you can produce at lower cost or higher quality outright.
Comparative advantage: you have lower opportunity cost for a specific activity.
Business implication:
• outsource or partner when others have comparative advantage
• specialize to become best option in a narrow segment, then expand

3) Competitive Dynamics
Sources of advantage:
• cost leadership (lower MC/AC)
• differentiation (higher WTP, lower elasticity)
• focus (targeting and distribution efficiency)
• switching costs and lock-in
• network effects (if applicable)
• learning curves and scale economies

4) Market Size: TAM, SAM, SOM
• TAM: total addressable market
• SAM: serviceable addressable market
• SOM: serviceable obtainable market
Bottoms-up sizing method:
1) count buyers in your ICP (region/industry/size)
2) estimate penetration rate at maturity
3) multiply by ACV or average spend
4) sanity check with competitor revenue and public benchmarks

Deliverables
1) market definition and substitute boundary
2) comparative advantage map (own vs outsource)
3) advantage hypothesis and evidence plan
4) TAM/SAM/SOM model with assumptions
