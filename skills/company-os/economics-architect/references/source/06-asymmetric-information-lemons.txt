ECONOMICS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 06: Information Economics: Asymmetric Information, Lemons, Moral Hazard, and Solutions

Purpose
Translate microeconomics and macroeconomics into operating decisions for building and scaling businesses.
This doctrine is action-oriented: pricing, incentives, product strategy, market selection, capacity planning,
competitive dynamics, and macro-aware forecasting.

Core Economic Lens for Business
1) Scarcity and tradeoffs: every choice has an opportunity cost.
2) Incentives and behavior: systems produce outcomes.
3) Margins: decisions happen at the margin (marginal cost vs marginal benefit).
4) Markets: prices coordinate supply and demand under constraints.
5) Information: asymmetric information creates failures and opportunities.
6) Externalities: costs/benefits often spill over beyond the transaction.
7) Cycles: macro conditions shift demand, costs, and risk.

Operating Rules
1) Always define the unit of analysis (customer, order, cohort, market segment, time period).
2) Convert narratives into numbers: demand curves, elasticities, unit economics, and constraints.
3) Separate price from value: price is a lever; value is the driver.
4) Use experiments to estimate uncertain parameters (elasticity, willingness to pay, churn response).
5) Keep models simple enough to guide decisions, strict enough to falsify.

============================================================
Scope
How information gaps create failures, and how businesses design trust systems.

1) Asymmetric Information
Forms:
• adverse selection: bad types select in
• moral hazard: behavior changes after contract
• principal-agent problem: incentives misaligned
Results:
• market failure, low trust, underinvestment

2) Lemons Problem
If buyers cannot tell quality, they pay an average price, pushing high-quality sellers out.
Business translation:
• new brands suffer a trust discount
• high-quality offers need proof to avoid the average-price trap

3) Screening and Signaling
Signaling: informed party credibly signals quality.
Examples:
• warranties, guarantees
• certifications, audits
• transparent case studies and benchmarks
• references
Screening: uninformed party designs mechanisms to reveal type.
Examples:
• trials with usage milestones
• tiered onboarding requiring effort
• application processes for high-touch service
• performance-based pricing where feasible

4) Trust Mechanisms
• escrow and milestones
• SLAs and penalties
• audits and logs
• reputational systems
• security posture and compliance evidence

Deliverables
1) asymmetric info map (where trust breaks)
2) signaling asset plan
3) screening mechanisms plan
4) risk-reduction checklist for sales and onboarding
