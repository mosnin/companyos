ECONOMICS ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 01: Economic Resources, Scarcity, Opportunity Cost, and Tradeoffs

Purpose
Translate microeconomics and macroeconomics into operating decisions for building and scaling businesses.
This doctrine is action-oriented: pricing, incentives, product strategy, market selection, capacity planning,
competitive dynamics, and macro-aware forecasting.

Core Economic Lens for Business
1) Scarcity and tradeoffs: every choice has an opportunity cost.
2) Incentives and behavior: systems produce outcomes.
3) Margins: decisions happen at the margin (marginal cost vs marginal benefit).
4) Markets: prices coordinate supply and demand under constraints.
5) Information: asymmetric information creates failures and opportunities.
6) Externalities: costs/benefits often spill over beyond the transaction.
7) Cycles: macro conditions shift demand, costs, and risk.

Operating Rules
1) Always define the unit of analysis (customer, order, cohort, market segment, time period).
2) Convert narratives into numbers: demand curves, elasticities, unit economics, and constraints.
3) Separate price from value: price is a lever; value is the driver.
4) Use experiments to estimate uncertain parameters (elasticity, willingness to pay, churn response).
5) Keep models simple enough to guide decisions, strict enough to falsify.

============================================================
Scope
Foundations: resources, tradeoffs, marginal thinking, rational choice, and decision discipline.

1) Economic Resources
A) Human resources
• time, skills, attention, coordination capacity, trust
B) Non-human resources
• capital (cash, credit), equipment, data, IP, brand, relationships, land, energy, compute
C) Constraint thinking
Your true limiting resource is the bottleneck: the scarcest input that limits output.

2) Opportunity Cost (Business Form)
Opportunity cost = the best alternative you give up.
Practical applications:
• choosing feature A means delaying feature B and revenue from B
• hiring role X means not hiring role Y
• allocating ad spend to channel A means not learning channel B

Opportunity cost checklist:
• what alternative is displaced
• what is the time horizon
• what is the “best alternative” given constraints
• what evidence would change the decision

3) Tradeoffs and the Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF)
PPF: feasible combinations of outputs given resources and technology.
Business translation:
• speed vs quality
• growth vs profitability
• customization vs standardization
Use:
• map your frontier today
• identify investments that shift the frontier outward (process, tooling, automation, training)

4) Marginal Thinking
Marginal benefit (MB): added benefit of one more unit.
Marginal cost (MC): added cost of one more unit.
Rule:
• do more while MB > MC
• stop or redesign when MC rises above MB (diminishing returns)

Examples:
• marketing spend: keep spending while incremental profit positive
• hiring: add headcount while incremental output exceeds fully-loaded cost
• product scope: add features only if incremental adoption or willingness-to-pay exceeds cost

5) Rational Choice Theory (Operational)
Assumption: agents choose options that maximize perceived utility under constraints.
Business use:
• model customers as utility maximizers with frictions (time, trust, risk)
• design offers that reduce friction and increase perceived payoff
• treat “irrationality” as missing variables (defaults, attention, trust)

Deliverables
1) constraint map (human + non-human)
2) opportunity cost decision template
3) PPF tradeoff map for your business
4) marginal decision rules for marketing, hiring, product
