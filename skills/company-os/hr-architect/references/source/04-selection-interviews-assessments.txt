HR / HIRING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 04: Selection Systems: Screening, Structured Interviews, Work Samples, Fairness

Purpose
Design and run a hiring and people system that scales: attract, select, onboard, develop, retain,
and offboard humans and contractors, while governing “digital workers” (AI agents/automation)
as part of the operating model.

Core People System
1) Plan: org design, roles, competencies, headcount, and success metrics.
2) Attract: sourcing channels, employer brand, and recruiting operations.
3) Select: structured evaluation, evidence-based decisions, and risk controls.
4) Close: offers, compensation, classification, and start logistics.
5) Enable: onboarding, training, documentation, and manager routines.
6) Perform: goals, feedback, growth, and performance improvement.
7) Retain: culture, engagement, comp fairness, and career pathways.
8) Exit: offboarding, knowledge capture, and alumni/referrals.
9) Operate: tools, compliance hygiene, analytics, continuous improvement.
10) Automate: AI agents as governed capacity, not unmanaged “magic”.

Operating Rules
1) Hiring is a system: improve conversion and quality at the constraint.
2) Define success upfront: scorecards prevent “vibes hiring”.
3) Use structured interviews and work samples whenever possible.
4) Track adverse impact risk and job-relatedness of selection procedures.
5) Documentation is leverage: SOPs + onboarding assets reduce dependence on tribal knowledge.
6) Contractors/VAs are a distinct operating model; classify and manage intentionally.
7) AI agents are “digital workers”: define scope, permissions, monitoring, and human approvals.

============================================================
Scope
Evidence-based selection: screens, structured interviews, assessments, work samples, and fairness controls.

1) Selection Design Principles
1) job-related evaluation (linked to role requirements)
2) structured process: same questions, same rubric
3) multiple signals: interview + work sample + references
4) documented decisions: reduce bias and improve learning

2) Structured Interviews
A structured interview uses:
1) questions derived from job analysis
2) standardized process across candidates
3) anchored rating scales and scoring rubrics

3) Work Samples and Simulations
Work samples are high-signal because they resemble the job.
Examples:
1) sales: draft a sequence + discovery roleplay
2) marketing: campaign plan + KPI tree
3) ops: process map + bottleneck analysis
Rules:
1) time-box scope
2) pay for large projects when appropriate
3) grade with a rubric tied to the scorecard

4) Screens and Assessments
Screen for must-haves and disqualifiers.
Assessment categories:
1) skills tests
2) case studies
3) work samples
Fairness controls:
1) validate relevance
2) provide accommodations where required
3) monitor selection rates by stage

5) Adverse Impact Monitoring
In the U.S., the Uniform Guidelines describe a “four-fifths” rule as an initial indicator for adverse impact in selection rates.
Track selection rates by stage and investigate disparities with additional evidence.

6) Decision Meetings
Decision hygiene:
1) independent scoring before discussion
2) discuss evidence, not charisma
3) require written rationale referencing scorecard outcomes
4) capture “win/loss” notes for process improvement

Deliverables
1) structured interview guides + rubrics
2) work sample templates + scoring sheets
3) selection process documentation + monitoring plan
4) decision meeting rules
