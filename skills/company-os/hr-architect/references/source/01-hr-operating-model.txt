HR / HIRING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 01: HR Operating Model and Talent Strategy

Purpose
Design and run a hiring and people system that scales: attract, select, onboard, develop, retain,
and offboard humans and contractors, while governing “digital workers” (AI agents/automation)
as part of the operating model.

Core People System
1) Plan: org design, roles, competencies, headcount, and success metrics.
2) Attract: sourcing channels, employer brand, and recruiting operations.
3) Select: structured evaluation, evidence-based decisions, and risk controls.
4) Close: offers, compensation, classification, and start logistics.
5) Enable: onboarding, training, documentation, and manager routines.
6) Perform: goals, feedback, growth, and performance improvement.
7) Retain: culture, engagement, comp fairness, and career pathways.
8) Exit: offboarding, knowledge capture, and alumni/referrals.
9) Operate: tools, compliance hygiene, analytics, continuous improvement.
10) Automate: AI agents as governed capacity, not unmanaged “magic”.

Operating Rules
1) Hiring is a system: improve conversion and quality at the constraint.
2) Define success upfront: scorecards prevent “vibes hiring”.
3) Use structured interviews and work samples whenever possible.
4) Track adverse impact risk and job-relatedness of selection procedures.
5) Documentation is leverage: SOPs + onboarding assets reduce dependence on tribal knowledge.
6) Contractors/VAs are a distinct operating model; classify and manage intentionally.
7) AI agents are “digital workers”: define scope, permissions, monitoring, and human approvals.

============================================================
Scope
How HR and hiring connect to strategy, and how to build an HR operating model even in small teams.

1) Talent Strategy
Start with business strategy inputs:
1) growth plan: revenue goals, products, markets
2) operating model: how work gets done (sales-led, product-led, services, hybrid)
3) constraints: time-to-market, quality, compliance, cash
Then translate into:
1) headcount plan (roles + timing)
2) capability plan (skills you must own vs buy)
3) leadership bandwidth plan (who manages whom)

2) HR Operating Models
Option A: founder-led HR (early)
1) simple processes, high context, fast iteration
2) risks: inconsistency, bias, missing documentation
Option B: ops-led HR (scale)
1) documented processes, scorecards, tooling
2) risks: bureaucracy if overbuilt
Option C: HRBP model (larger)
1) HR partners embedded by business unit, metrics-driven enablement

3) Core Metrics
Leading indicators:
1) time-to-first-response to applicants
2) applicant to screen conversion
3) screen to interview conversion
4) interview to offer conversion
5) offer acceptance rate
6) time-to-productivity
Lagging indicators:
1) 90-day retention
2) performance distribution at 6–12 months
3) regretted attrition
Quality of hire (practical composite):
1) manager satisfaction at 90 days
2) objective output milestones hit
3) retention at 6–12 months

4) Hiring Flywheel
Define, Source, Screen, Evaluate, Close, Onboard, Perform, Refer.
For each stage define:
1) owner
2) SLA
3) assets/templates
4) metrics
5) review cadence

Deliverables
1) talent strategy one-pager
2) headcount and capability plan
3) hiring flywheel with SLAs
4) metrics dashboard spec
