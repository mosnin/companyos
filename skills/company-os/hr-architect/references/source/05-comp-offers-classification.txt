HR / HIRING ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 05: Compensation, Offers, Worker Classification, Contractors, EOR/PEO

Purpose
Design and run a hiring and people system that scales: attract, select, onboard, develop, retain,
and offboard humans and contractors, while governing “digital workers” (AI agents/automation)
as part of the operating model.

Core People System
1) Plan: org design, roles, competencies, headcount, and success metrics.
2) Attract: sourcing channels, employer brand, and recruiting operations.
3) Select: structured evaluation, evidence-based decisions, and risk controls.
4) Close: offers, compensation, classification, and start logistics.
5) Enable: onboarding, training, documentation, and manager routines.
6) Perform: goals, feedback, growth, and performance improvement.
7) Retain: culture, engagement, comp fairness, and career pathways.
8) Exit: offboarding, knowledge capture, and alumni/referrals.
9) Operate: tools, compliance hygiene, analytics, continuous improvement.
10) Automate: AI agents as governed capacity, not unmanaged “magic”.

Operating Rules
1) Hiring is a system: improve conversion and quality at the constraint.
2) Define success upfront: scorecards prevent “vibes hiring”.
3) Use structured interviews and work samples whenever possible.
4) Track adverse impact risk and job-relatedness of selection procedures.
5) Documentation is leverage: SOPs + onboarding assets reduce dependence on tribal knowledge.
6) Contractors/VAs are a distinct operating model; classify and manage intentionally.
7) AI agents are “digital workers”: define scope, permissions, monitoring, and human approvals.

============================================================
Scope
Close hires predictably: comp architecture, offers, negotiation, and correct worker classification.

1) Compensation Architecture
Components:
1) base pay
2) variable pay (commission, bonus)
3) equity (where applicable)
4) benefits and stipends
Principles:
1) pay for scope and impact
2) define bands per level and role family
3) protect internal equity with consistent leveling
4) tie variable to controllable metrics

2) Offer Process
1) verbal alignment on key terms
2) written offer letter and role outcomes
3) deadline and follow-up plan
Include:
1) mission and outcomes
2) compensation and benefits
3) start date and location/remote terms
4) expectations for first 30/60/90 days

3) Employee vs Contractor Classification
Classification depends on the relationship and degree of control and independence, often grouped as:
1) behavioral control
2) financial control
3) relationship of the parties
Misclassification risk is material. Use a checklist before engaging contractors or VAs.

4) Contractors and VAs as an Operating Model
Use for:
1) clear deliverables
2) limited control over how work is performed
3) project-based or capacity-based support with defined outputs
Controls:
1) statement of work (SOW)
2) security and data access limits
3) QA and acceptance criteria
4) payment terms tied to milestones

5) Global Hiring Options
1) contractor model
2) Employer of Record (EOR)
3) Professional Employer Organization (PEO)
Choose based on desired control, compliance, speed, and cost.

Deliverables
1) comp bands and leveling matrix
2) offer checklist and negotiation rules
3) contractor vs employee classification checklist
4) SOW template for VAs/contractors
5) global hiring decision tree
