SALES ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 02: Prospecting: ICP, Lead Sources, List Building, and Prioritization

Purpose
Design and run a sales system that reliably converts attention into revenue. This doctrine covers:
prospecting, outreach, appointment setting, qualification, discovery, pitching, objection handling,
closing, referrals, and scaling through process + tools + management.

Core Sales System (End-to-End)
1) Targeting: define ICP + buying triggers + reachable channels.
2) Prospecting: build and prioritize lead lists with evidence-based intent signals.
3) Outreach: run a multi-channel cadence to earn a conversation.
4) Qualification: confirm fit, urgency, authority, and economic impact.
5) Discovery: quantify pain and map decision process.
6) Proposal/Demo: present a tailored solution with proof and clear next steps.
7) Negotiation/Close: reduce risk, remove friction, finalize terms, get signature.
8) Post-Sale: drive success, expansion, referrals, and durable relationships.

Operating Rules
1) Activity is a leading indicator; conversion is the constraint.
2) Sales is structured learning: every step produces data that improves the next step.
3) Control is earned via clarity: agenda, next steps, and mutual commitments.
4) Qualification is disqualification. Saying “no” early increases win rate.
5) Pipeline is a model. Forecasting improves when stage definitions are strict.

============================================================
Scope
How to build a prospecting engine: ICP, triggers, list sources, enrichment, prioritization, and lead routing.

1) ICP: The “Who” That Makes Sales Easy
ICP dimensions (choose the few that matter):
• Firmographics: industry, size, geography, revenue, headcount
• Technographics: tools in use (stack)
• Workflow maturity: “how they do it today”
• Constraints: compliance, timelines, budgets
• Buying motion: self-serve vs sales-led
• Success profile: who retains and expands

ICP Output: A 1-paragraph ICP statement + 5 disqualifiers.

2) Buying Triggers and Intent Signals (The “Why Now”)
Examples:
• hiring growth
• new funding or budget cycle
• leadership change
• product launch
• compliance deadline
• outage or incident
• cost-cutting mandate
• competitor switch
• public initiative (press releases)
Classify triggers:
A) structural triggers (slow)
B) event triggers (fast)
C) behavioral triggers (high intent: inbound actions)

3) Lead Sources
Owned
• existing customers (expansion)
• referrals
• newsletter/community
Inbound
• website leads, content, SEO, webinars
Outbound
• directories, databases, job postings, press releases, event attendee lists
Partnerships
• agencies, consultants, vendors with adjacency

4) List Building and Enrichment
Minimum viable lead record:
• company, website, industry, size
• primary contact, title, email, phone, LinkedIn
• hypothesis: pain + trigger + why now
• first-touch channel and personalization angle

5) Prioritization: Scoring Without Fantasy
Priority score = Fit × Urgency × Access
Fit
• matches ICP and disqualifier filters
Urgency
• trigger evidence + timeline signal
Access
• reachable decision-maker path

6) Lead Routing (How Work Moves)
Routing rules:
• inbound high intent: immediate response SLA
• outbound: sequence assignment by segment
• recycle: closed lost with nurture path
• no response: park in long-cycle nurture

Deliverables
1) ICP statement + disqualifiers
2) trigger library for your category
3) lead record schema
4) prioritization score model
5) routing rules (inbound/outbound/recycle)
