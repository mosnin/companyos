SALES ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 03: Cold Outreach Systems: Phone, Email, LinkedIn, DM, and Cadences

Purpose
Design and run a sales system that reliably converts attention into revenue. This doctrine covers:
prospecting, outreach, appointment setting, qualification, discovery, pitching, objection handling,
closing, referrals, and scaling through process + tools + management.

Core Sales System (End-to-End)
1) Targeting: define ICP + buying triggers + reachable channels.
2) Prospecting: build and prioritize lead lists with evidence-based intent signals.
3) Outreach: run a multi-channel cadence to earn a conversation.
4) Qualification: confirm fit, urgency, authority, and economic impact.
5) Discovery: quantify pain and map decision process.
6) Proposal/Demo: present a tailored solution with proof and clear next steps.
7) Negotiation/Close: reduce risk, remove friction, finalize terms, get signature.
8) Post-Sale: drive success, expansion, referrals, and durable relationships.

Operating Rules
1) Activity is a leading indicator; conversion is the constraint.
2) Sales is structured learning: every step produces data that improves the next step.
3) Control is earned via clarity: agenda, next steps, and mutual commitments.
4) Qualification is disqualification. Saying “no” early increases win rate.
5) Pipeline is a model. Forecasting improves when stage definitions are strict.

============================================================
Scope
Multi-channel outbound built for meetings: cadences, scripts, personalization, follow-ups, and compliance basics.

1) The Outbound Equation
Meetings = Prospects × (Contact rate) × (Conversation rate) × (Meeting conversion)
Improve by isolating the constraint:
• low contact rate: data quality and channel choice
• low conversation rate: opener and relevance
• low meeting conversion: offer + qualification and control

2) Cadence Design (Multi-Channel)
A cadence is a planned sequence of touches across channels with a goal per step.
Cadence principles:
• one clear ask per message
• follow-ups add value or new angle
• vary channel and format
• keep intervals consistent
Cadence template (example)
Day 1: email 1
Day 2: call + voicemail
Day 4: LinkedIn connect + short note
Day 6: call
Day 8: email 2 (new proof)
Day 12: email 3 (low-commitment CTA)
Day 16: breakup email + permission to close loop

3) Cold Call Frameworks
A) Permission-based opener
1) introduce yourself and reason for calling
2) ask for 20–30 seconds
3) deliver relevance line
4) ask a simple question
B) Problem-first opener
1) name common pain in their role
2) ask if relevant
C) Trigger-based opener
1) reference a real trigger
2) connect to likely outcome
3) ask for quick confirmation

Cold call structure
1) opener (earn attention)
2) relevance (why them)
3) question (discover)
4) value (what you change)
5) close for meeting (next step)

4) Cold Email Architecture
Subject: neutral and specific
Body:
1) personalization or trigger (1 line)
2) problem hypothesis (1 line)
3) credibility/proof (1 line)
4) clear CTA (one option)
5) signature
Follow-up rule:
• do not “bump”
• add proof, insight, or alternative CTA

5) LinkedIn and DMs
Principles
• short, context-first
• do not pitch immediately after connecting
• use questions that reduce commitment
Sequence: connect → value note → question → invite to call

6) Networking as Pipeline Creation
Networking outputs:
• introductions
• credibility transfer
• insider info on decision process
Rules
• give first (introductions, insights)
• keep a “referral loop” running
• track relationships like accounts (CRM)

7) Compliance Basics (Outbound)
Email rules differ by country. In the U.S., commercial email must follow CAN-SPAM: clear identification, truthful headers/subjects,
a visible opt-out, and honoring opt-outs promptly. (Do not treat this as legal advice; implement compliance per your jurisdiction.)

Deliverables

2) cold call scripts by opener type
3) cold email template library
4) LinkedIn/DM sequences
5) networking system and tracking fields
6) compliance checklist
