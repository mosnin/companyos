SALES ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 10: Sales Operations, Quotas, Forecasting, Slumps, and Management

Purpose
Design and run a sales system that reliably converts attention into revenue. This doctrine covers:
prospecting, outreach, appointment setting, qualification, discovery, pitching, objection handling,
closing, referrals, and scaling through process + tools + management.

Core Sales System (End-to-End)
1) Targeting: define ICP + buying triggers + reachable channels.
2) Prospecting: build and prioritize lead lists with evidence-based intent signals.
3) Outreach: run a multi-channel cadence to earn a conversation.
4) Qualification: confirm fit, urgency, authority, and economic impact.
5) Discovery: quantify pain and map decision process.
6) Proposal/Demo: present a tailored solution with proof and clear next steps.
7) Negotiation/Close: reduce risk, remove friction, finalize terms, get signature.
8) Post-Sale: drive success, expansion, referrals, and durable relationships.

Operating Rules
1) Activity is a leading indicator; conversion is the constraint.
2) Sales is structured learning: every step produces data that improves the next step.
3) Control is earned via clarity: agenda, next steps, and mutual commitments.
4) Qualification is disqualification. Saying “no” early increases win rate.
5) Pipeline is a model. Forecasting improves when stage definitions are strict.

============================================================
Scope
Systems that scale sales: CRM design, lead storage, forecasting, quotas, performance management, and leadership.

1) Where to Store Leads (Data System)
Avoid “inbox CRM”. Use a single source of truth:
A) CRM (recommended): HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive, Close, etc.
B) Spreadsheet (temporary): must mirror CRM fields and be migrated.
Minimum fields
• contact and company identifiers
• source and segment
• last touch and next step date
• stage and stage-entered date
• notes (discovery, objections, stakeholders)
• deal value and close date estimate

2) CRM and Pipeline Hygiene
Rules
• every deal must have next step scheduled or be closed/recycled
• stage changes require evidence notes
• no “Proposal sent” without a follow-up meeting on calendar
Dashboards
• meetings booked and held
• stage conversion rates
• cycle time by stage
• win/loss reasons

3) Forecasting Methods (Practical)
• Stage-weighted pipeline: probability by stage × deal value
• Historical conversion: volume × win rate × ACV
• Commit/best-case/pipeline bands: conservative forecasting categories
Forecast hygiene
• stage definitions strict
• close dates updated weekly
• probabilities tied to evidence, not optimism

4) Quotas, Activity Targets, and Slumps
Quota math
Quota attainment ≈ (activities × conversion rates × deal size)
To fix a slump:
1) check list quality (fit and triggers)
2) check activity volume (outreach and follow-ups)
3) check meeting conversion (scripts)
4) check stage conversion (discovery and proof)
5) check close (stakeholders, MAP, paper process)

5) Scaling Scripts and Playbooks
Formats
• “call script” (openers, questions, closes)
• “talk track” (themes, not verbatim)
• “objection matrix” (objection × response × proof)
• “sequence library” (email/call/LinkedIn cadence)
Store in:
• a shared playbook doc + version control
• CRM snippets/templates (where supported)
• call recording library with tagged examples

6) Moving Into Management
Manager responsibilities
• hiring and onboarding
• territory and quota design
• pipeline reviews and forecasting accuracy
• coaching via call reviews
• process enforcement and continuous improvement
Coaching cadence
• weekly 1:1 (skills + pipeline)
• bi-weekly deal reviews
• monthly skill training
• quarterly performance and territory review

Deliverables
1) CRM schema and required fields
2) dashboards and reporting spec
3) forecasting model and definitions
4) slump diagnosis checklist
5) playbook storage and versioning plan
6) management cadence and coaching templates
