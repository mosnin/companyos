SALES ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 07: Objections and Negotiation: Risk, Resistance, and Decision Dynamics

Purpose
Design and run a sales system that reliably converts attention into revenue. This doctrine covers:
prospecting, outreach, appointment setting, qualification, discovery, pitching, objection handling,
closing, referrals, and scaling through process + tools + management.

Core Sales System (End-to-End)
1) Targeting: define ICP + buying triggers + reachable channels.
2) Prospecting: build and prioritize lead lists with evidence-based intent signals.
3) Outreach: run a multi-channel cadence to earn a conversation.
4) Qualification: confirm fit, urgency, authority, and economic impact.
5) Discovery: quantify pain and map decision process.
6) Proposal/Demo: present a tailored solution with proof and clear next steps.
7) Negotiation/Close: reduce risk, remove friction, finalize terms, get signature.
8) Post-Sale: drive success, expansion, referrals, and durable relationships.

Operating Rules
1) Activity is a leading indicator; conversion is the constraint.
2) Sales is structured learning: every step produces data that improves the next step.
3) Control is earned via clarity: agenda, next steps, and mutual commitments.
4) Qualification is disqualification. Saying “no” early increases win rate.
5) Pipeline is a model. Forecasting improves when stage definitions are strict.

============================================================
Scope
Handle objections as information, then negotiate without destroying trust or margin.

1) Objections: Categories
A) No pain: “We don’t need this.”
B) No trust: “Not sure you can deliver.”
C) No urgency: “Not now.”
D) No authority: “I’m not the decision maker.”
E) No money: “No budget.”
F) No fit: “Not the right solution.”

2) Objection Handling Frameworks
A) LAER
Listen → Acknowledge → Explore → Respond
B) Clarify then confirm
• “When you say X, do you mean Y or Z?”
C) Label + question
• “Sounds like timing is the concern. What’s driving that?”
D) Evidence ladder
Claim → proof → peer proof → guarantee/risk reversal

3) Negotiation Fundamentals (Professional)
• BATNA: best alternative to a negotiated agreement
• ZOPA: zone of possible agreement
• Trade, don’t concede: every discount gets a term (longer contract, upfront payment, reduced scope)
• Protect referenceability: bad-fit deals create churn and brand damage

4) Pricing and Discount Policy
Define in advance:
• discount bands and required approvals
• standard give-get trades
• non-negotiables (scope, payment terms, deliverables)

5) Procurement and Paper Process
Expect:
• vendor onboarding
• security and compliance questionnaires
• legal review
• payment terms negotiation
Treat paper process as a stage with owners and dates.

Deliverables
1) objection library by category with best responses
2) negotiation playbook: BATNA, give-get trades, approval rules
3) pricing policy sheet
4) procurement checklist + timeline
