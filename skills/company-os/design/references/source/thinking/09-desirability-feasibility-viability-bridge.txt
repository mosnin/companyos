DESIGN THINKING DOCTRINE — SECTION 09: Desirability–Feasibility–Viability Bridge

Purpose
Build a repeatable, professional-grade design thinking system that converts ambiguous user reality into validated solutions.
This is a knowledge and execution reference. It focuses on method, structure, and artifacts.

Core Outputs
1) Clear artifacts (canvases, maps, hypotheses, tests)
2) Evidence (observations, measurements, decisions)
3) Iteration loop (learn → update → retest)

Operating Rules
1) User truth beats team opinion
2) Behavior beats self-report when they conflict
3) Fast loops beat perfect plans
4) Evidence updates the model, not ego
5) Make uncertainty explicit, then attack it

============================================================
Scope
Prevent building things that are wanted but unbuildable, buildable but unwanted, or wanted but not sustainable.

A. Desirability
Questions
1) Real job and pain?
2) Alternative clearly worse?
3) Meaningful improvement quickly?
4) Concrete commitment?

Evidence
• Behavioral tests
• Switching with tradeoffs
• Repeat usage

B. Feasibility
Questions
1) Build credible version quickly?
2) Operate reliably?
3) Dependencies and risks?
4) Minimum architecture for quality?

Evidence
• Prototype completion
• Reliability indicators
• Cost and time grounded in capability

C. Viability
Questions
1) Sustainable economics?
2) Acquisition and delivery scale?
3) Revenue streams and constraints?
4) Advantage or moat?

Evidence
• Unit economics assumptions and sensitivity
• Pricing and willingness to pay tests
• Sales cycle reality

D. Bridging Method
1) Map assumptions in each dimension
2) Rank by risk
3) Test highest risk first
4) Update Lean Canvas and Customer Profile

Deliverables
1) Assumption map across D, F, V
2) Risk-ranked top assumptions
3) Test plan per assumption
4) Decision rules: continue, pivot, stop
