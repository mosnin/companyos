DESIGN ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 05: Knowledge in the World vs Knowledge in the Head

Purpose
Apply the principles of "The Design of Everyday Things" to digital product design so the right action is obvious
and the wrong action is hard. Produce design decisions, UI structures, copy, interaction patterns, and tests
that reduce the gulfs of execution and evaluation.

Non‑negotiable thesis
Make the right action obvious and the wrong action hard.

============================================================
5) Knowledge in the world vs knowledge in the head

5.1 Knowledge in the world
1) External cues, labels, layout, instructions, signifiers.
2) Benefits:
1) Reduces memory burden
2) Supports recognition over recall
3) More resilient under stress

5.2 Knowledge in the head
1) Memory, learned procedures, internalized conventions.
2) Risks:
1) Users forget
2) Users misremember
3) Users transfer habits incorrectly from other systems

Design direction:
1) Prefer knowledge in the world when possible.
2) Use knowledge in the head when it is a stable, widely shared convention.

Implementation guidance
A) Recognition over recall defaults
• show examples and defaults
• autocomplete and suggestions
• visible history and recent items
• inline definitions and tooltips where needed

B) When to rely on knowledge in the head
• platform conventions (common icons, gestures, shortcuts)
• domain conventions for expert tools (only if audience is expert)
Rule: if the audience is mixed, expose knowledge in the world.

C) Stress test
Under time pressure, users revert to recognition, not memory.
Design for the stress case, not the calm case.

Deliverables
1) recall to recognition conversion checklist
2) onboarding map: what must be taught vs what is conventional
3) contextual help policy: when and where help appears
