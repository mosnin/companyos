DESIGN ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 02: The Two Gulfs That Define Usability

Purpose
Apply the principles of "The Design of Everyday Things" to digital product design so the right action is obvious
and the wrong action is hard. Produce design decisions, UI structures, copy, interaction patterns, and tests
that reduce the gulfs of execution and evaluation.

Non‑negotiable thesis
Make the right action obvious and the wrong action hard.

============================================================
2) The two “gulfs” that define usability

2.1 Gulf of execution
Gap between:
1) What the user wants to do
2) What actions the user can perceive are possible
Design objective: make possible actions visible and understandable.

2.2 Gulf of evaluation
Gap between:
1) What the system did
2) What the user can perceive and interpret the result to mean
Design objective: make system state visible, legible, and interpretable.

Implementation guidance
A) Execution gulf reducers
• show primary action first
• provide signifiers at the moment of choice
• reduce decision load via defaults and progressive disclosure
• prevent dead ends with constraints and guided flows

B) Evaluation gulf reducers
• show state changes immediately
• show “what changed” plus “what to do next”
• make feedback proportional to action cost
• expose system status in a stable location (status bar, progress, history)

C) Diagnostic questions
• if a user fails, is it because they could not find what to do?
• or because they could not tell what happened after they acted?

Deliverables
1) gulf diagnosis worksheet per flow step
2) execution cues plan (signifiers, hierarchy, defaults)
3) evaluation cues plan (status, feedback, recovery)
