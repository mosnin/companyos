DESIGN ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 08: Conventions, Culture, and Naturalness

Purpose
Apply the principles of "The Design of Everyday Things" to digital product design so the right action is obvious
and the wrong action is hard. Produce design decisions, UI structures, copy, interaction patterns, and tests
that reduce the gulfs of execution and evaluation.

Non‑negotiable thesis
Make the right action obvious and the wrong action hard.

============================================================
8) Conventions, culture, and “naturalness”

8.1 Conventions are learned, but powerful
1) Many “intuitive” interfaces are intuitive only because they match conventions.
2) Breaking conventions requires compensating signifiers and onboarding.

8.2 Semantic meaning matters
1) Controls should match the meaning of the action and context.
2) Grouping and labeling should reflect real world categories users expect.

Implementation guidance
A) Convention decision rule
• follow platform defaults unless there is a measurable reason not to
• if breaking, add signifiers, preview, and onboarding
• test comprehension with first‑time users

B) Semantic grouping
• group by user goal, not by internal architecture
• name groups by user outcomes

Deliverables
1) convention audit: which patterns you follow and where you intentionally diverge
2) grouping and labeling plan mapped to user mental categories
