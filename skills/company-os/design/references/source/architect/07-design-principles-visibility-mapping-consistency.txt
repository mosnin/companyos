DESIGN ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 07: Principles That Fall Out of the Above

Purpose
Apply the principles of "The Design of Everyday Things" to digital product design so the right action is obvious
and the wrong action is hard. Produce design decisions, UI structures, copy, interaction patterns, and tests
that reduce the gulfs of execution and evaluation.

Non‑negotiable thesis
Make the right action obvious and the wrong action hard.

============================================================
7) Design principles that fall out of the above

7.1 Visibility of system status
1) Users should always be able to answer:
1) Where am I
2) What state is the system in
3) What can I do next
4) What just happened

7.2 Good signifiers over “more instructions”
1) If you need a label that explains a control’s existence, the signifier failed.
2) Instructions are last resort and should be contextual.

7.3 Natural mapping as default
1) Arrange controls to mirror outcomes.
2) Use spatial layouts and direct manipulation where possible.

7.4 Consistency and standards
1) Internal consistency: same action behaves the same everywhere.
2) External consistency: follow platform and cultural conventions.

7.5 Constraints as guidance
1) Use constraints to narrow choices and indicate correct paths.
2) Good constraints feel like clarity, not restriction.

7.6 Feedback tuned to action cost
1) High cost actions require strong feedback and reversibility.
2) Low cost actions require lightweight feedback.

Implementation guidance
A) Status visibility pattern set
• breadcrumbs or location indicator
• persistent state indicator (draft, saved, synced)
• activity log or recent events
• progress indicator for multi-step flows

B) Consistency rules
• one verb, one meaning (create vs add vs new)
• one color meaning (error, warning, success)
• one interaction model per component

Deliverables
1) system status spec for your product
2) component consistency checklist
3) feedback strength rubric by action cost
