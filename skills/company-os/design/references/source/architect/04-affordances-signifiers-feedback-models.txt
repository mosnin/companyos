DESIGN ARCHITECT DOCTRINE — SECTION 04: Fundamental Interaction Ingredients

Purpose
Apply the principles of "The Design of Everyday Things" to digital product design so the right action is obvious
and the wrong action is hard. Produce design decisions, UI structures, copy, interaction patterns, and tests
that reduce the gulfs of execution and evaluation.

Non‑negotiable thesis
Make the right action obvious and the wrong action hard.

============================================================
4) The fundamental interaction ingredients

4.1 Affordances
1) What actions are physically or digitally possible.
2) Affordances are real possibilities, not labels.
Key point:
1) An affordance that cannot be perceived is useless.

4.2 Signifiers
1) Perceivable cues that indicate where actions are possible and how to do them.
2) Examples: a handle shape, a raised button, a highlighted link, a “+” icon.
Rule:
1) Designers design signifiers, not affordances.

4.3 Feedback
1) Information returned to the user about what action was taken and what happened.
2) Feedback must be:
1) Immediate enough to connect cause and effect
2) Informative enough to explain state
3) Proportional to the importance of the action

4.4 Conceptual models
1) The user’s mental explanation of how the system works.
2) Good design supplies a good conceptual model through:
1) Structure
2) Language
3) Mappings
4) Feedback
5) Consistency

4.5 Mappings
1) The relationship between controls and their effects.
2) Good mapping is:
1) Natural
2) Spatially compatible
3) Consistent with common conventions

4.6 Constraints
Constraints limit actions to prevent error and guide behavior.
Four main types:
1) Physical constraints
2) Semantic constraints (meaning based)
3) Cultural constraints (conventions)
4) Logical constraints (reasoning, layout, exclusivity)

Implementation guidance
A) Affordance and signifier audit
• list every intended action
• verify the signifier exists, is perceivable, and implies the correct action
• remove false signifiers that suggest unavailable actions

B) Feedback ladder by action cost
• low cost: subtle confirmation
• medium cost: explicit state change + brief message
• high cost: strong confirmation + undo path + audit entry

C) Constraints as “guardrails”
• prevent invalid inputs inline
• disable impossible actions with an explanation
• use previews and interlocks for irreversible actions

Deliverables
1) affordance‑signifier map per screen
2) feedback spec per action (timing, content, modality)
3) constraint catalog and where each is applied
