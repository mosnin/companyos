# Evidence Research Campaign

## Campaign funnel

1. Frame one decision question, not a vague topic.
2. Map independent research lanes: primary sources, practitioner voices, customer/user signal, counterevidence, and neglected sources.
3. Discover broadly using metadata and snippets; deduplicate before opening full sources.
4. Prioritize sources by information gain, independence, recency, credibility, directness, contradiction value, and cost.
5. Extract located evidence with source, author, date, context, claim, and limitation.
6. Verify material claims independently; deliberately search for disconfirming evidence.
7. Synthesize into a decision report.

## Report structure

- Question and decision context.
- Executive recommendation and confidence.
- Evidence table: claim, source, evidence type, freshness, independence, limitation.
- What real users/practitioners report, separated from vendor claims.
- Counterevidence and unresolved disagreements.
- Technical/business implications, options, costs, risks, and next experiment.
- Research coverage, cutoff, budget used, blind spots, and invalidation triggers.

## Guardrails

Use direct links and dates. Do not treat search snippets, social proof, copied press releases, or repeated claims as independent evidence. Respect access restrictions, privacy, and platform terms. Stop when the decision threshold is met or new lanes yield little novelty.
