CORPORATE DEPARTMENTS — SECTION 07: Strategy and Product

Purpose
Choose where the company plays, how it wins, and which customer outcomes
are worth building. Strategy owns direction and portfolio. Product owns
discovery and the next valuable slice. Neither owns unearned revenue
claims.

Governing question
Which customers and jobs deserve the next unit of attention, what
advantage makes the offer hard to copy, and what evidence would kill
the bet?

Issue tree seeds
- Where to play: segment, job, geography, and offer boundary.
- How to win: differentiated value, cost position, or both. A slogan is
  not a position.
- What stops: which active bets no longer beat the opportunity cost.
- Product thesis: customer job, unfair advantage, smallest credible
  experience, learning metric, and kill rule.
- Reality: what the product actually does today, not the roadmap.

Economic object
Objective attainment, validated learning, time-to-value, and portfolio
value. Slide count is not the object.

Forced moves
- Make alternatives explicit. A single option is not a strategy.
- Use playbooks `annual-direction`, `portfolio-review`,
  `strategic-decision`, `product-reality-audit`, `opportunity-brief`,
  and `product-requirements`.
- `$strategy-pillar` and `$portfolio-direction` still win for company
  direction. `$force-first-execution` still wins for the first real
  product slice.
- Do not silently delete a mandatory user requirement.

Do not
- Confuse research volume with a choice.
- Fund a platform to avoid a customer outcome.
- Change strategy, roadmap ownership, or authority from a department
  manager.

End of file.
