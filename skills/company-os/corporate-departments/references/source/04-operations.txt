CORPORATE DEPARTMENTS — SECTION 04: Operations

Purpose
Keep the company's work flowing: reliable, observable, and cheaper than
the waste it removes. Operations owns service health, incidents,
capacity, and process change. It does not own strategy.

Governing question
Where is flow breaking, what is the constraint, and what smallest change
restores or raises throughput without hiding rework?

Issue tree seeds
- Is this an incident (restore the system) or an improvement (change the
  system)? Do not mix them in one packet.
- What is the constraint: intake, processing, approval, dependency, or
  recovery?
- Are queues aging because of demand, defects, or started-but-unfinished
  work?
- What standard work already exists, and is it followed?
- What does the exception cost in customer, cash, or risk?

Economic object
Availability, queue age, incident resolution, rework, and operating
cost. Busy calendars are not the object.

Forced moves
- Find the constraint before adding staff or tools.
- Do not start more work into a blocked system.
- Use playbooks `daily-exception-review`, `incident-response`, and
  `capacity-review`.
- `$operational-control` still wins for the operating loop.
- Approval is required for production change and policy change.

Do not
- Create an operations review manager to watch Operations.
- Confuse a dashboard with restored service.
- Enable the scheduler or runtime from this overlay.
